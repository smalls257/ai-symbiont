## Question

Plug-and-play for a new user: a Llama 3.2 1B memory model is meta-trained once, on public repos, to answer from lessons placed in its prompt. Does that adapter (a2) recall an unseen repo's lessons better than the plain 1B model given the same lessons in the prompt (a1), with no training for the new repo? A lesson is one failed command, its error and the command that fixed it, mined from the public nebius OpenHands trajectories (CC-BY-4.0) by the LCAP lesson builder (`scripts/routea/lessons.py`). The claim covers this recipe and these repos only. It makes no claim about speed of learning.

Those two repos are excluded from the scored set.
- **The feasibility a2 adapter is discarded and never scored.** Its meta-train held out only those two repos, so it saw all 13 scored repos [measured: `held.json` lists 2 repos]. The scored a2 is trained once, from the meta-train list below.
- `results/warm-start-habits.json` (exploratory, committed with the warm-start PR) trained per-repo adapters from plain 1B on the 15 repos with 10 or more distinct lessons [measured], so the per-repo-adapter reference's recall on the 13 scored repos is already known. No a1, a2 or control number exists for them. The scored run trains the reference arm again under this file's recipe.

## Recipe (frozen as in the feasibility run)

- Base: mlx-community Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct bf16, laptop MLX.
- a2 meta-train: 3 lessons per prompt (`WINDOW = 3`), every LCAP training pair per lesson, and one abstain row per lesson ("No lesson I hold covers that.") with another meta-train repo's lessons in the prompt. LoRA through `scripts/routea/train_lora.sh` (rank 16, scale 2, all layers, learning rate 1e-4 cosine, batch 4, max length 512, seed 0), 1 epoch.
- Per-repo adapter: `train_lora.sh` from plain 1B on the repo's LCAP training rows (`questions.training_messages`), 8 epochs, seed 0.
- **Decoding.** `scripts/routea/answer_questions.py` at the freeze commit: greedy decoding, `MAX_TOKENS = 96`, chat-template date fixed at "26 Jul 2024", the whole answer folded onto one line (since `ef0954a`), batch 16. Answers from any other setting are not pooled with these.
- One training seed per arm.

## Probes

- **Reworded templates unseen in training.** `questions.held_out_questions`: five fixed rewordings per lesson (25 probes at 5 lessons, 50 at 10). No training row uses them [established: `meta_rows` and `training_messages` use only `_training_pairs`]. These are fixed template rewordings, not paraphrases of lesson text.
- **Verbatim.** The six training wordings per lesson, reported beside them, never deciding.

## Metrics

1. **New-lesson recall.** The LCAP scorer (`scoring.score`) on the reworded probes, per repo; verbatim beside it.
2. **Confabulation under wrong lessons.** An answer to repo R's probe with repo W's lessons in the prompt confabulates if, for some W lesson, every token of `added` is present, or the `fix` string is present, AND that lesson's (error class, `added`) key is in no R lesson. Text is compared after `scoring._QUOTES` stripping and whitespace folding exactly as `recalled` does (`plug.confabulates`). Rate = confabulating answers / probes, per repo, at 5 lessons (primary) and 10 (secondary).
3. **Abstention.** An answer abstains if, folded, it starts with "No lesson I hold covers that" or contains "no lesson" in any case (`plug.abstains`). The abstention rate under wrong lessons and the false-abstention rate under right lessons are reported for every arm.
4. **Habits kept, by ranked choice** (`rank_habits.py`). The 44 AIDev held-out habit questions (sha256 `78d2267d767202737d21b215bcfaa74ad0413a2a0c72f20d968843a8b22c3b1f`), with the habit system prompt `scripts/aidev/training.SYSTEM` (file sha256 `c4678461a047be012a1ea9287c7ac66f73aa26cad8dd0c87c2f1469d603ed2e8`) [measured]. Each question scores the five kinds' check sentences (`scripts/aidev/lessons.CHECK`) as the assistant reply, using only the reply tokens, by mean log-likelihood per token (a sum would favor short sentences). A hit means the question's own kind ranks strictly first; a tie for first is a miss. Chance is 0.20. Exploratory values: plain 1B 9/44, habits adapter 31/44, the feasibility a2 adapter 35/44 [measured]. This check was adopted after the exploratory keyword score was seen (post hoc there); this prereg fixes it as primary before any scored data [measured: order of work]. The keyword score (`score_habits.py`) is printed beside it as secondary.
5. **Invented-detail rate** from the LCAP scorer, secondary.

All per-repo numbers are paired by repo and summarized as the mean over repos with a bootstrap interval from `warm_start.paired_gain` [established]:
10,000 resamples of repos with replacement, seed 0, 95% percentile interval [established].

## Hypotheses and expected width

- **H1 (primary):** a2 minus a1, reworded-probe recall at 5 lessons, mean over 13 repos. Expected: `better` [hypothesis]. The feasibility gap was +0.32 on one repo [measured, exploratory].
- Width: a per-repo recall rests on 25 probes, so the per-repo gain has a standard deviation of about 0.15 to 0.25 once repo spread is added [hypothesis]. Over 13 repos the 95% interval half-width is about 0.10 to 0.12 [hypothesis]. A true gain under about 0.10 is expected to read `tie`, and the percentile bootstrap at n = 13 under-covers slightly [established]. Thirteen repos suffice only if the feasibility gap holds at a third of its size; that is the bet.

## Decision rule (fixed before data)

- **Primary: H1.** Gain = a2 − a1 per repo. **better** if the interval's lower bound is above 0 and the mean gain is at least 0.05; **worse** if the upper bound is below 0; otherwise **tie**.
- **Secondary**, same words and thresholds: a2 vs a1 at 10 lessons; a2 vs the per-repo adapter at 5 and at 10 lessons.
- **Confabulation control.** a2 passes only if (a) a1 minus a2 (confabulation, per repo, 5 lessons) is not `worse`, and (b) a2's mean confabulation rate at 5 lessons is at most 0.10 with the interval's upper bound below 0.20 [hypothesis; the owner may lower these numbers before the freeze, never raise them after]. An a2 that is `better` on recall but fails the control is reported as "recalls more and confabulates more", never as a win.
- **Habits.** a2 and the per-repo adapter against plain 1B, no decision. If a2 is below plain 1B the report says "recalls more and keeps fewer habits" (feasibility keyword score: 0.41 against 0.50 [measured]).
- **"Plug-and-play works"** is claimed only if all three hold: H1 `better`, the confabulation control passes, and a2 minus the per-repo adapter at 5 lessons is not `worse`. The wording is then "recalls X of the reworded probes with 5 lessons in the prompt on 13 unseen repos, against Y for the plain model, without per-repo training". Anything less reads "a meta-trained reader did not measurably beat the plain model at this size", with the interval. No speed-of-learning words.
- No other comparison decides. Every row is printed, including floors and controls.

## Known limits

- One training seed per arm; seed variance is not estimated.
- Thirteen mid-size repos with 10 or more distinct lessons do not represent the pool.
- The 1,300-character drop removes long lessons and biases the meta-train set toward short fixes (333 rows dropped [measured]); rows beyond the 1,600 cap are dropped by shuffle order (seed 0).
- The a2 meta-train prompt holds 3 lessons while the probe prompt holds 5 or 10. The feasibility drop from 0.60 to 0.48 at 10 lessons is consistent with that shift [measured, one repo].
- The scorer is token containment, so an answer that quotes the whole prompt scores a recall hit. Only the confabulation and invented-detail rows catch that.
- Multiplicity: H1 is one interval; the secondaries are three (a2 vs a1 at 10; a2 vs the adapter at 5 and 10); confabulation adds two (a1 minus a2, and the absolute bar). That is about six 95% intervals with no correction [established], plus verbatim, invented-detail, habits and sensitivity tables, which never decide.
